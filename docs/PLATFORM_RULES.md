# Platform rules and monetization checklist

Researched 2026-11-17 from the platforms' own policy pages. Policies change and enforcement is done by
classifiers and reviewers, so this lowers risk; it cannot guarantee an outcome. Re-check the linked pages
before applying to any monetization program. Items marked **(unverified)** could not be read on the live
official page and need a manual check.

## 1. Rules we build every video to

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|------|-----|
| 1 | Length 61-90 seconds | TikTok Creator Rewards only pays on videos over 1 minute |
| 2 | Narrator voice must be licensed for commercial use | The first voice used (`en_US-ryan`) is CC BY-NC-SA: non-commercial. Now `en_US-bryce` (public domain); `en_US-joe` (CC0) is the fallback |
| 3 | All visuals, music and sound effects are generated by our own code | Avoids copyright claims and "reused / unoriginal content" rules on all four platforms |
| 4 | Upload the clean master file to each platform. Never re-upload a download from another app | Watermarks from other apps cut reach on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok |
| 5 | Every part has a distinct story beat, new shots and a different ending | YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule rejects channels that look templated or mass-produced |
| 6 | No firearms on screen. Threats use tools or bare hands | TikTok makes content showing firearms ineligible for the For You feed and age-restricts it |
| 7 | No gore, no visible wounds, no bodies, no lingering on an injury. Harm is brief, non-graphic or off screen | Graphic fictional violence: TikTok For You ineligible, YouTube limited ads, Meta age-gated |
| 8 | No jump scares (sudden loud audio + flash) | TikTok lists jump scares as For You ineligible |
| 9 | No child characters in danger | TikTok bans "endangerment of young people" with no stated exception for fiction |
| 10 | No real people, real events or real wars | Triggers AI-disclosure rules and Meta's "tragedy or conflict" restrictions |
| 11 | No suicide or self-harm depiction | Age-restricted and For You ineligible on TikTok |
| 12 | Never offer prizes or rewards for comments, likes or follows | Banned as incentivized engagement on YouTube, TikTok and Meta |
| 13 | State "fiction" in the caption | YouTube keeps dramatized violence up when the metadata shows it is fictional |
| 14 | Titles, captions and cover frames stay non-graphic and are not misleading | Advertiser rules apply to the title and thumbnail too |

## 2. The "comment A or B" ending

- **YouTube:** allowed. Encouraging comments is explicitly permitted; only rewards or deception are not.
- **TikTok:** allowed. No rule against asking for comments; only tricking or bribing people is restricted.
- **Facebook / Instagram: risk.** Meta's engagement-bait rules define *comment baiting* as "asking people
  to comment with specific answers (words, numbers, phrases, or emojis)" and *vote baiting* as asking
  people to vote by comment. Posts are demoted, repeat pages are demoted more, and engagement bait is
  listed as a prohibited behaviour in the Content Monetization Policies. There is no stated exception for
  interactive stories. **(wording seen in search excerpts of the official pages; unverified live)**

What we do: `python make_video.py --soft <episode>` renders a Meta cut (`*-meta.mp4`). It shows the same
two choices but removes the A/B letters and replaces "COMMENT A OR B" with "WHAT WOULD YOU DO?". The
narration never says "comment". Use the standard cut on YouTube and TikTok, the Meta cut on Facebook and
Instagram. Other creators do post "comment A or B" on Facebook; that is their risk to take.

## 2b. The end card that names the other platforms

Every video ends with a card showing the channel name, the handle and the words Instagram, Facebook, YouTube
and TikTok. Platforms demote videos that carry another app's **logo or watermark** (Instagram and Facebook say so
explicitly; TikTok treats another app's watermark as unoriginal content). Plain text names are not covered by
those rules, so the card uses text only and never a logo. Whether any platform quietly reduces reach for
naming a competitor is not documented either way. If reach on one platform looks suppressed, the first thing
to test is a cut whose end card shows only the handle.

## 3. Upload settings

| Platform | Setting |
|----------|---------|
| YouTube | Audience: **Not made for kids** (a made-for-kids flag removes comments, which breaks voting). No "altered content" disclosure needed: fully animated, non-realistic content is exempt |
| TikTok | No AI label needed: generic text-to-speech and stylised animation are explicitly exempt. Personal account, not Business |
| Facebook / Instagram | Turn the **AI info** label on. Meta requires it for "realistic-sounding audio" that is digitally generated, and our narrator is synthetic. The label does not reduce reach by policy |

## 4. Monetization programs

| Platform | Program | Requirements |
|----------|---------|--------------|
| YouTube | Partner Program, ad revenue | 1,000 subscribers + 10M Shorts views in 90 days (or 4,000 watch hours). Shorts keep 45% of allocated revenue. Reported to rise to 20M Shorts views for new applicants from 1 Feb 2027 **(from a YouTube blog post; verify)** |
| YouTube | Partner Program, fan funding | 500 subscribers, 3 uploads in 90 days, 3M Shorts views in 90 days |
| TikTok | Creator Rewards Program | 18+, US personal account, 10,000 followers, 100,000 views in 30 days. Video over 1 minute, original, at least 1,000 For You views |
| Facebook | Content Monetization | Invite-only. Request it in Professional Dashboard > Monetization. No published threshold |
| Instagram | Gifts, Subscriptions, Bonuses | No general ad revenue share on Reels. Bonuses are invite-only. Thresholds not published **(unverified)** |

A Short over 1 minute with any Content ID claim is blocked on YouTube, which is another reason all audio is ours.

## 6. How this shapes the consequence system

Consequences are costs the story can show without breaking the rules above: a limp, lost air, a locked
door, lost time, lost trust. The pursuer threatens with a tool, not a gun. A fall or a blow is shown as a
quick, non-graphic beat and the camera moves on. Deaths, if any, happen off screen.
